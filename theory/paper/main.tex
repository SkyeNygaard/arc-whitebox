\begin{abstract}
We study static, network-independent linear cubature for Gaussian
expectations of a bias-free ReLU network in the infinite-width limit.
Positive homogeneity reduces the problem to integration on
\(\mathbb{S}^{255}\), and ensemble mean-squared error becomes discrepancy
for the normalized depth-32 ReLU kernel.

A complete Kerdock system of 129 real mutually unbiased bases gives an
equal-weight rule with 66,048 antipodal nodes. Among all nonnegative
mass-one rules on arbitrary spherical nodes with at most this budget, its
risk is at most 0.0233242\% above the infimum. Even with arbitrary signed
mass-one weights, no static rule can reduce Kerdock risk by more than
6.2940\%.

The nonnegative bound uses an all-degree Delsarte certificate. Its auxiliary
optimum is a degree-five Hermite minorant. The signed bound uses harmonic
comparison profiles, matrix inertia, and an exact-rational coefficient
allocation. Together, the bounds limit what fixed node geometry can achieve.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

Consider the Gaussian expectation
\[
\mu(f)=\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_d)}[f(X)].
\]
When \(f\) is a deep network, each evaluation may be expensive. Monte
Carlo applies broadly. Deterministic cubature instead evaluates \(f\) at
fixed inputs and returns a weighted average.

This paper asks how far that strategy can be improved when the rule is
chosen without access to the realized network. The nodes and weights are
fixed in advance. The estimator is linear in the observed outputs. The
budget is the number of full network evaluations.

The reference design comes from a maximal system of real mutually unbiased
bases in dimension 256. The Kerdock construction contains 129 bases.
Including every basis vector and its antipode gives 66,048 spherical nodes.
Its pairwise inner products take only five values, so its kernel risk can
be evaluated directly.

The bounds apply to arbitrary spherical nodes. They do not assume a
Kerdock structure, antipodal symmetry, equal weights, or any other special
geometry. Among nonnegative mass-one rules, Kerdock is at most
0.0233242\% above the infimum. Allowing arbitrary real weights that sum to
one enlarges the class, but no such static rule can reduce Kerdock risk by
more than 6.2940\%.

The nonnegative theorem nearly determines the best achievable risk over
probability rules for this kernel and budget. The signed theorem limits the
benefit of cancellation to 6.2940\%. Any larger gain must leave the class.
It could use the realized network, adaptive evaluations, nonlinear
processing, or a different evaluation-cost model.

An all-degree Delsarte program gives the nonnegative lower bound. Harmonic
moment matrices and an inertia inequality give the signed lower bound.
Both certificates combine directed interval arithmetic with exact integer
and rational replay.

\section{Problem setting}\label{problem-setting}

\subsection{From Gaussian input to spherical integration}
\label{from-gaussian-input-to-spherical-integration}

Let \(f:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}\) be positively homogeneous of degree
one:
\[
f(rx)=r f(x), \qquad r\geq 0.
\]
Write a standard Gaussian vector as \(X=RU\). Here \(U\) is uniform on
\(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\), and \(R=\lVert X\rVert_2\) is independent of \(U\).
If \(\sigma\) is normalized surface measure, then
\[
\mathbb{E}[f(X)]
=\mathbb{E}[R]\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}} f(u)\,d\sigma(u).
\]
Bias-free ReLU networks satisfy this homogeneity exactly. The radial
factor is known, so the numerical problem is spherical integration. We
report risk in the spherical normalization. For the original Gaussian
expectation, ensemble mean-squared error is multiplied by
\(\mathbb{E}[R]^2\). All risk ratios are unchanged.

\subsection{The deep ReLU kernel}\label{the-deep-relu-kernel}

Let \(K_{32}\) be the normalized depth-32 ReLU correlation kernel. It is
the Gaussian-process kernel of a fully connected, bias-free, infinite-width
network. The activation is \(\phi(z)=\sqrt{2}\max\{0,z\}\), or equivalently
ordinary ReLU with He variance scaling. We use a centered Gaussian readout.
Depth 32 means 32 applications of the covariance map. For unit vectors,
\[
\mathbb{E}[F(u)F(v)] = K_{32}(u^\top v),
\]
where
\[
K_0(t)=t, \qquad K_{\ell+1}(t)=\kappa(K_\ell(t)),
\]
and
\[
\kappa(t)=\frac{\sqrt{1-t^2}+(\pi-\arccos t)t}{\pi}.
\]
This is the standard compositional ReLU kernel
\citep{cho2009,daniely2016,lee2018,matthews2018}.

The kernel is positive definite on the sphere. It therefore has a
normalized Gegenbauer expansion
\[
K_{32}(t)=\sum_{\ell\geq 0} a_\ell G_\ell(t),
\qquad a_\ell\geq 0,
\qquad G_\ell(1)=1.
\]
The coefficient \(a_\ell\) is the total kernel weight assigned to
spherical harmonic degree \(\ell\).

\subsection{Static linear cubature}\label{static-linear-cubature}

A rule with at most \(N\) consolidated nodes is
\[
Q(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i f(u_i),
\qquad n\leq N,
\qquad u_i\in\mathbb{S}^{255},
\qquad \sum_{i=1}^{n}w_i=1.
\]
The nodes and weights are independent of the realized network and of all
observed function values. We consider
\[
\mathcal{C}_{+}=\{Q:w_i\geq0\},
\qquad
\mathcal{C}_{\pm}=\{Q:w_i\in\mathbb{R}\}.
\]

Define the ensemble mean-squared integration error
\[
\mathcal{R}(Q)
=\mathbb{E}\left[
\left(Q(F)-\int F\,d\sigma\right)^2
\right].
\]
Because \(Q\) is fixed before the Gaussian field is drawn,
\[
\mathcal{R}(Q)
=\iint K_{32}(u^\top v)\,d(\sigma-Q)(u)\,d(\sigma-Q)(v).
\]
Thus the integration error is exactly a spherical kernel discrepancy.

\subsection{The complete Kerdock rule}\label{the-complete-kerdock-rule}

A real mutually unbiased system in dimension \(d\) has at most
\(d/2+1\) bases. Kerdock constructions attain this bound in dimension
\(d=256\), giving
\[
B=d/2+1=129
\]
orthonormal bases \citep{calderbank1997,boykin2005}. Vectors from
different bases have inner-product magnitude \(1/16\).

The complete Kerdock rule \(Q_{\mathrm K}\) uses every basis vector and
its antipode with equal weight. It has
\[
N=2Bd=66{,}048
\]
nodes. Among ordered pairs, \(N\) have inner product \(1\), \(N\) have
inner product \(-1\), and \(4Bd(d-1)\) have inner product \(0\). The
values \(1/16\) and \(-1/16\) each occur \(2B(B-1)d^2\) times. This
spectrum reduces \(\mathcal{R}(Q_{\mathrm K})\) to five kernel evaluations
and the constant Gegenbauer coefficient.

\subsection{Related work}\label{related-work}

Delsarte linear programming, positive-definite zonal kernels, and spherical
designs provide the framework for the nonnegative bound
\citep{schoenberg1942,delsarte1977,cohn2007}. Weighted spherical energy
bounds give a nearby general theory \citep{borodachov2024weighted}. Our
result fixes both the potential and the budget: the depth-32 ReLU kernel
and 66,048 nodes.

Kerdock codes and real mutually unbiased bases are linked to extremal line
sets and association schemes
\citep{calderbank1997,boykin2005,abdukhalikov2009}. Exact semidefinite
programming also proves packing optimality for the 66,048-point,
dimension-256 antipodal Kerdock arrangement \citep{cohn2024sdp}. Packing
and kernel discrepancy are different objectives.

Kernel quadrature studies integration through reproducing-kernel
discrepancy and Gaussian-process uncertainty
\citep{briol2019}. Here the goal is a finite-budget lower
bound that holds for every rule in a fixed estimator class.

\section{Main results}\label{main-results}

Let \(P_N\) be the infimum of \(\mathcal{R}(Q)\) over
\(\mathcal{C}_{+}\) with at most \(N\) consolidated nonzero-weight nodes.
Let \(S_N\) be the corresponding infimum over \(\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\).

\textbf{Theorem 1 (Nonnegative near-optimality).}
\emph{For the dimension-256, depth-32 kernel and \(N=66{,}048\),}
\[
1\leq
\frac{\mathcal{R}(Q_{\mathrm K})}{P_N}
\leq 1.0002332417295004.
\]
\emph{Thus Kerdock risk is at most 0.023324172950039\% above the
infimum. The statement is one-sided. Kerdock may attain the infimum, and
no optimizer is assumed to exist.}

\textbf{Theorem 2 (Signed bound).}
\emph{For the same kernel and node budget,}
\[
S_N
\geq 0.9370601683665084\,\mathcal{R}(Q_{\mathrm K}).
\]
\emph{Equivalently,}
\[
\frac{\mathcal{R}(Q_{\mathrm K})}{S_N}
\leq 1.0671673322143325.
\]
\emph{A static signed mass-one rule can therefore reduce Kerdock risk by
at most 6.29398316334916\%.}

Both results allow arbitrary spherical node locations. The first allows
arbitrary nonnegative weights. The second allows arbitrary real weights
that sum to one. Neither theorem assumes symmetry or equal weights.

The signed certificate strengthens as the number of negative support
entries grows. With at least 1,072 such entries, the Kerdock-to-rule risk
factor is below 1.05. With at least 4,160, the rule's certified lower bound
exceeds the certified Kerdock upper bound. These count support entries, not
total negative mass. Appendix~\ref{app:signed} derives the hierarchy and
lists representative values.

\section{The nonnegative bound}\label{the-nonnegative-bound}

The proof of Theorem~1 uses a Delsarte minorant and a dual quadrature rule.
The certificate controls every harmonic degree.

\subsection{Auxiliary minorants}\label{auxiliary-minorants}

Let
\[
h(t)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}c_\ell G_\ell(t)
\]
be a polynomial such that
\[
h(t)\leq K_{32}(t) \quad (-1\leq t\leq1),
\qquad
c_\ell\geq0 \quad (\ell\geq1).
\]
For every nonnegative mass-one rule,
\[
\sum_{i,j}w_iw_jG_\ell(u_i^\top u_j)\geq0
\qquad (\ell\geq1),
\]
and
\[
\sum_i w_i^2\geq\frac1N.
\]
Apply \(h\) to the off-diagonal pairs and retain the exact value
\(K_{32}(1)\) on the diagonal. The kernel energy
\[
E_K(Q)=\sum_{i,j}w_iw_jK_{32}(u_i^\top u_j)
\]
then satisfies
\[
E_K(Q)
\geq c_0+\frac{K_{32}(1)-h(1)}{N}.
\]
Subtracting the constant kernel mean gives a lower bound on
\(\mathcal{R}(Q)\).

The auxiliary program maximizes
\[
\Phi(h)=c_0+\frac{K_{32}(1)-h(1)}{N}
\]
over feasible polynomial minorants. The all-degree dual certificate shows
that no omitted polynomial degree can improve this objective.

\subsection{The optimal minorant}\label{the-optimal-minorant}

Let \(t_1,t_2,t_3\) be the roots of
\[
22102t^3+21930t^2-87t-85=0.
\]
Let \(h_\star\) be the degree-five Hermite interpolant defined by
\[
h_\star(t_j)=K_{32}(t_j),
\qquad
h_\star'(t_j)=K_{32}'(t_j),
\qquad j=1,2,3.
\]

\textbf{Theorem 3 (All-degree auxiliary optimum).}
\emph{The polynomial \(h_\star\) is a feasible minorant. Its five
nonconstant normalized Gegenbauer coefficients are strictly positive. It
is the unique optimizer among feasible polynomial minorants.}

The certificate has four parts.

\subsubsection{Exact dual quadrature}\label{exact-dual-quadrature}

The objective \(\Phi\) defines a linear functional with rational moments
through degree five. Its monic degree-three orthogonal polynomial is the
cubic above. Exact root intervals and Lagrange formulas give a positive
three-node quadrature rule that is exact through degree five.

\subsubsection{All higher degrees}\label{all-higher-degrees}

An exact integer recurrence checks degrees 6 through 14,658. A normalized
Gegenbauer tail bound covers every larger degree. Every omitted degree has
strictly negative reduced cost. Higher-degree terms cannot improve the
auxiliary objective.

\subsubsection{Global minorant}\label{global-minorant}

A directed derivative certificate proves
\[
K_{32}^{(6)}(t)>0
\qquad (-1<t<1).
\]
The Hermite remainder formula gives
\[
K_{32}(t)-h_\star(t)
=\frac{K_{32}^{(6)}(\xi_t)}{6!}
\prod_{j=1}^{3}(t-t_j)^2
\geq0.
\]
Separate directed evaluations verify both endpoints.

\subsubsection{Optimality and uniqueness}\label{optimality-and-uniqueness}

A Krawczyk enclosure places all five nonconstant Gegenbauer coefficients
strictly above zero. Contact at the three quadrature nodes makes the
primal and dual objectives equal. Strict reduced costs force every
optimizer to have degree at most five. Dual equality forces contact at all
three interior nodes, and differentiability then forces derivative
contact. The six Hermite conditions determine \(h_\star\) uniquely.

The resulting directed lower bound is
\[
\mathcal{R}(Q)
\geq 2.4330928587565938\times10^{-7}
\qquad (Q\in\mathcal{C}_{+}).
\]
Comparison with the certified Kerdock-risk interval proves Theorem~1.
Appendix~\ref{app:nonnegative} records the exact roots, endpoint margins,
and certified constants.

\section{The signed bound}\label{the-signed-bound}

Negative weights can cancel harmonic moments, so the nonnegative Delsarte
argument no longer applies directly. The signed proof instead controls the
inertia of harmonic moment matrices.

\subsection{A positive-index inequality}\label{a-positive-index-inequality}

For one comparison profile, let \(E\) be its node-evaluation matrix and
let
\[
W=\operatorname{diag}(w_i),
\qquad
M=E^\top W E.
\]
For the profiles used below, the addition formula fixes the trace
\(T>0\) of \(M\) and gives a target matrix \(A\) with squared Frobenius
norm \(S_2\). It also gives
\[
\lVert A-M\rVert_F^2=\lVert M\rVert_F^2-S_2.
\]

\textbf{Lemma 4 (Positive-index bound).}
\emph{If a symmetric matrix \(M\) has trace \(T>0\) and at most \(p\)
positive eigenvalues, then}
\[
\lVert M\rVert_F^2\geq\frac{T^2}{p}.
\]

\emph{Proof.} Let \(\lambda_1^+,\ldots,\lambda_p^+\) be the positive
eigenvalues. Their sum is at least \(T\). Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\lVert M\rVert_F^2
\geq\sum_i(\lambda_i^+)^2
\geq\frac{(\sum_i\lambda_i^+)^2}{p}
\geq\frac{T^2}{p}.
\qquad\square
\]

Congruence cannot increase positive inertia. After duplicate nodes are
consolidated and zero weights removed, a rule with \(q\) negative support
entries has at most \(N-q\) positive weights. Hence
\[
\lVert A-M\rVert_F^2
\geq\frac{T^2}{N-q}-S_2.
\]

\subsection{The harmonic witness}\label{the-harmonic-witness}

The certificate uses 146 comparison profiles. Each profile parameter is
nonnegative. The witness uses kernel coefficients through degree 320, and
its coefficient allocation is fixed and rational. Higher degrees contribute
nonnegative squared harmonic discrepancies and may be discarded. After the
directed coefficient lower endpoints are supplied, every remaining step
uses exact rational arithmetic.

A mass-one rule requires a case split. If all consolidated weights are
nonnegative, Theorem~1 gives the stronger bound. Otherwise \(q\geq1\), so
every profile matrix has positive index at most \(N-1\). Applying the
frozen allocation gives
\[
\mathcal{R}(Q)
\geq 2.2804861843861462\times10^{-7}.
\]
Combining the two cases proves Theorem~2. Replacing \(N-1\) by \(N-q\)
gives the negative-support hierarchy.

Appendix~\ref{app:signed} derives the comparison-kernel identity and the
exact coefficient-allocation inequality. The ancillary certificate lists
all 146 profiles and verifies every rational slack. The bound is one-sided;
it makes no claim that a signed optimizer exists or attains the floor.

\section{Interpretation}\label{interpretation}

Theorem~1 leaves about two parts in ten thousand between Kerdock and the
nonnegative infimum. For this kernel and budget, changing only fixed nodes
and nonnegative weights offers almost no further reduction in ensemble
risk.

Theorem~2 is less tight, but it covers all real mass-one weights. Static
signed cancellation cannot reduce Kerdock risk by more than 6.2940\%.
Crossing this boundary requires something outside the theorem. Examples
include finite-width or network-specific structure, adaptive queries,
nonlinear reconstruction, and cheaper approximate evaluations.

The broader contribution is methodological. The proof links a neural
Gaussian-process kernel to spherical discrepancy. It then combines an
all-degree Delsarte certificate with an inertia bound for signed atomic
measures. This gives a template for studying other fixed kernels and node
budgets.

\section{Scope and reproducibility}\label{scope-and-reproducibility}

The theorem concerns the normalized depth-32 infinite-width kernel, not a
realized finite-width network. Nodes and weights must be fixed independently
of the network and observed values. Aggregation must be linear. Signed
weights must sum to one. The budget counts full function evaluations. It
does not equalize preprocessing cost or wall time. It also does not account
for instability from large signed weights. Both headline bounds are
one-sided. They do not construct an optimizer or prove that either lower
bound is attained.

The ancillary directory is a minimal proof archive. It contains the
all-degree recurrence, the degree-five primal--dual records, the Kerdock
risk check, the frozen signed witness, and the inertia strengthening. Exact
replay verifies all integer and rational steps downstream of the stored
directed interval endpoints. Independent high-precision calculations also
reproduce the formal series, Hermite interpolant, and Kerdock risk.

The complete depth-32 Gegenbauer coefficient and curvature interval stack
has not been independently rebuilt in a separate directed-arithmetic
implementation. The artifacts therefore support a replayable
computer-assisted proof with an explicit trust base. They do not constitute
independent verification of every interval input. Appendix~\ref{app:verification}
maps the proof objects and commands.

\section{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

We proved class-wide lower bounds for static, network-independent linear
cubature of a deep ReLU Gaussian-process kernel. At 66,048 nodes, the
complete Kerdock rule is at most 0.0233242\% above the nonnegative
infimum. In the larger signed mass-one class, no rule can reduce Kerdock
risk by more than 6.2940\%.

The first result nearly determines the best nonnegative risk over arbitrary
nodes and weights. The second rules out a large hidden gain from signed
weights. Together they separate improvements available from fixed geometry
from improvements that require a different source of information or a
different estimator class.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}cc@{}}

\end{center}

E_K(Q_{\mathrm K})=

 +(B-1)d\{K(d^{-1/2})+K(-d^{-1/2})\}}{N}.
\]

\section{Details of the nonnegative certificate}\label{app:nonnegative}

For $N=66{,}048$, define the auxiliary functional on normalized
Gegenbauer polynomials by
\[
L(G_0)=1-\frac1N,
\qquad
L(G_\ell)=-\frac1N\quad(\ell\ge1).
\]
Its moments through degree five determine the monic orthogonal cubic
\[
p(t)=t^3+\frac{255}{257}t^2-\frac{87}{22102}t-
\frac{85}{22102}.
\]
Equivalently, $22102p(t)=22102t^3+21930t^2-87t-85$. Its three roots lie
in the disjoint rational intervals
\[
\begin{split}
 t_1&\in[-0.992278935,-0.992278934],\\
 t_2&\in[-0.062224856,-0.062224855],\\
 t_3&\in[\phantom{-}0.062285891,\phantom{-}0.062285892].
\end{split}
\]
The associated three-node Gaussian quadrature has positive weights. It is
exact through degree five. The degree-five Hermite interpolant $h_\star$ at
these roots is primal--dual optimal once feasibility and all omitted
reduced-cost signs are established.

The all-degree certificate has three components.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Exact integer recurrence proves every reduced cost is strictly negative
for degrees $6$ through $14{,}658$. A normalized-Gegenbauer tail estimate
covers every degree $\ell\ge14{,}659$.
\item A directed derivative argument proves $K_{32}^{(6)}(t)>0$ on
$(-1,1)$. The Hermite remainder is therefore nonnegative, with equality only
at the three contact roots in the interior.
\item Directed endpoint evaluation gives
\[
K_{32}(1)-h_\star(1)>0.01702189426837098,
\qquad
K_{32}(-1)-h_\star(-1)>2.2051871290807434\times10^{-7}.
\]
A Krawczyk enclosure places every nonconstant Gegenbauer coefficient of
$h_\star$ strictly above zero.
\end{enumerate}

The certified nonnegative floor and the Kerdock upper endpoint are
\[
P_N\ge2.4330928587565937917\times10^{-7},
\qquad
\mathcal R(Q_{\mathrm K})\le2.4336603575430052277\times10^{-7},
\]
which yield Theorem~1. The certificate optimizes the polynomial auxiliary program. It does not
assert that a spherical rule attains the resulting lower bound.

\section{Signed comparison profiles and the frozen witness}\label{app:signed}

Choose real orthonormal spherical harmonics $Y_{\ell m}$ so that the
addition formula is
\[
\sum_{m=1}^{d_\ell}Y_{\ell m}(x)Y_{\ell m}(y)
=d_\ell G_\ell(x^\top y),
\]
where $d_\ell=\dim H_\ell$. Let
$Y_\ell(x)=(Y_{\ell1}(x),\ldots,Y_{\ell d_\ell}(x))^\top$. For a
profile $(s,r)$ with $r\ge0$, set
\[
\Phi_{s,r}(x)=Y_s(x)\oplus\sqrt r\,Y_{s+1}(x),
\quad
A_{s,r}=I_{d_s}\oplus rI_{d_{s+1}},
\]
and
\[
L_{s,r}(t)=d_sG_s(t)+r d_{s+1}G_{s+1}(t).
\]
For a mass-one signed rule $Q=\sum_iw_i\delta_{u_i}$, define
\[
M_{s,r}=\sum_iw_i\Phi_{s,r}(u_i)\Phi_{s,r}(u_i)^\top.
\]
The sphere average of $\Phi_{s,r}(x)\Phi_{s,r}(x)^\top$ is
$A_{s,r}$. The addition formula and $\sum_iw_i=1$ fix the two
diagonal-block traces. Consequently, with
\[
T_{s,r}=d_s+r d_{s+1},
\qquad
S_{s,r}=d_s+r^2d_{s+1},
\]
one has
\[
\|A_{s,r}-M_{s,r}\|_F^2
=\mathcal R_{L_{s,r}^2}(Q)
=\|M_{s,r}\|_F^2-S_{s,r}.
\]
Here $\mathcal R_H(Q)$ denotes the discrepancy obtained by replacing
$K_{32}$ with the kernel $H$. If $M_{s,r}$ has at most $p$ positive
eigenvalues, Lemma~4 gives the
profile floor
\[
B_{s,r}(p)=\frac{T_{s,r}^2}{p}-S_{s,r}.
\]

Write the exact Gegenbauer linearization as
\[
L_{s,r}(t)^2=\sum_{\ell\ge0}b_{s,r,\ell}G_\ell(t),
\qquad b_{s,r,\ell}\ge0.
\]
The coefficients are nonnegative because $L_{s,r}^2$ is a positive-definite
zonal kernel.
The ancillary certificate stores 146 profiles $(s_j,r_j)$ and positive
rational allocations $y_j$. Write $B_j(p)=B_{s_j,r_j}(p)$. If
$a_\ell^{\rm lo}$ is the directed lower endpoint for the kernel coefficient,
the exact verifier checks
\[
\sum_j\frac{y_j}{B_j(N)}b_{j\ell}
\le a_\ell^{\rm lo}
\qquad(1\le\ell\le320).
\]
All arithmetic after the one-sided coefficient inputs is rational. Each
profile polynomial has finite Gegenbauer support. Unused kernel coefficients
are nonnegative. Hence, for any rule whose profile matrices have positive
index at most $p$,
\[
\mathcal R(Q)
\ge
\sum_j y_j\frac{B_j(p)}{B_j(N)}.
\]

After duplicate locations are consolidated and zero weights are removed, a
genuinely signed rule with $q$ negative support entries has at most $N-q$
positive weights. Congruence with $W=\operatorname{diag}(w_i)$ gives
$p\le N-q$. The universal theorem is a case split. The case $q=0$ is
covered by the stronger nonnegative theorem. The case $q\ge1$ uses
$p=N-1$. The frozen allocation then gives
\[
\mathcal R(Q)\ge2.2804861843861462175\times10^{-7}
\]
and the ratio in Theorem~2.

Using $p=N-q$ gives the support-count hierarchy stated in
Section~\ref{main-results}. The ancillary archive contains the full
exact-rational table. These are support-count statements, not bounds on
total negative mass. The certificate is a lower bound only. It does not
prove that a signed optimizer exists or attains the floor.
