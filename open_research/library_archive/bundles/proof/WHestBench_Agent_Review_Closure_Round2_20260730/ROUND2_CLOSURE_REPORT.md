## Executive result

The second closure pass found that the first package was already outdated in two directions.

First, the fixed Kerdock-line theorem is stronger than previously recorded: it extends exactly to every finite width and finite depth for the standard Gaussian-first-layer random-network ensemble. This is not a limiting-kernel transfer argument. Conditioning on later randomness and expanding the network as a Hilbert-valued function of the Gaussian first-layer preactivations gives a noise-stability kernel

\[
K_m(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nt^n,\qquad a_n\ge0.
\]

After antipodal line symmetrization, only even powers remain. The resulting three MUB association values satisfy the exact signs required by the general support-extremality theorem. Consequently, arbitrary real mass-one weights on the fixed MUB/Kerdock line universe are optimized by complete bases plus at most one partial basis, with equal positive weights inside active bases and positive analytic basis masses.

Second, the proposed universal cascade/observability impossibility route is invalid for the actual challenge output. The last activation is post-ReLU, so the observed random field is not Gaussian. Gaussian posterior-mean and Gaussian no-adaptation theorems cannot be used to claim that nonlinear, adaptive, or trajectory-using algorithms are closed. An explicit one-neuron ReLU ridge shows the failure: antipodal basis observations nonlinearly recover the coefficient norm and hence the exact integral, while a fixed linear average generally does not.

The correct final division is therefore:

- arbitrary-node, nonnegative T22 near-optimality: certified only for the depth-32 infinite-width kernel;
- fixed complete-support linear weighting: exact at every width under zonal ensemble symmetry;
- arbitrary real weighting and deletion inside the fixed Kerdock/MUB line universe: exact at every finite width and depth under the Gaussian-first-layer ensemble;
- nonlinear, adaptive, changed-support, and arbitrary-node signed methods: not closed universally.

## New theorem closures

### T29 — all-width complete-support fixed-linear theorem

For a finite Kerdock support whose ensemble Gram matrix has constant row sum, the quadratic ensemble risk is invariant under the transitive support symmetry. Symmetrizing any fixed weight vector gives a uniform vector without increasing risk. Thus uniform mass-one weighting is optimal at every width for every square-integrable zonal second-moment kernel generated by the ensemble.

If total mass is free, the optimum is an alpha-scaled uniform rule. For the archived depth-32 limiting-kernel constants,

\[
\alpha_*=0.999999750324728644\ldots
\]

and the relative risk reduction versus mass one is only

\[
2.4967527136\times10^{-7}.
\]

The theorem does not cover data-dependent weights, changed support, or nonlinear aggregation.

### T38 — exact finite-width Kerdock-line theorem

For every finite width and depth of a nondegenerate standard Gaussian-first-layer ReLU network, the symmetrized line kernel has nonnegative even power coefficients. If

\[
A=\overline K_m(1),\quad O=\overline K_m(0),\quad C=\overline K_m(1/\sqrt d),
\]

then

\[
A-O>0,\qquad O-C<0,
\]

and

\[
(A-O)+d(O-C)>0
\]

whenever the even output contains a Hermite component of degree at least four, which holds for a nondegenerate piecewise-linear ReLU output under the stated model.

Applying T37 gives the full finite-width fixed-line conclusion. This closes the previous finite-width qualification on T27 but not arbitrary-node finite-width T22.

### T39 — invariant-information projection theorem

If a compact or finite group acts measure-preservingly on instances, the error transforms unitarily, and runtime observations are group invariant, then the conditional mean error belongs to the invariant subspace. Runtime correction value is bounded by the squared norm of that invariant component. Under a proved sign-reversal symmetry with no invariant error component, every correction based only on orientation-blind Gram/norm information has zero expected alignment.

This is an exact theorem. Its WHestBench application remains conditional because no exact measure-preserving symmetry has yet been proved for the actual legal observation map.

### T40 — equivariant residual spectral multiplier

For an isotropic field and a deterministic bounded rotation-equivariant linear surrogate acting by scalar multiplier \(\tau_\ell\) on harmonic degree \(\ell\), the residual kernel coefficients are

\[
q_\ell^{\mathrm{res}}=|1-\tau_\ell|^2q_\ell.
\]

This gives an exact recertification recipe for a frozen common residual kernel. It does not cover nonlinear, candidate-dependent, or non-equivariant controls.
