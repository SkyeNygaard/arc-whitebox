Independent Short-Suffix Micro-Cubature for Sparse Late-Layer Anchors
Executive decision

CONTINUE one tightly scoped experiment. Do not model the full 256-dimensional activation distribution.

The best candidate is:

Externally Centered Adjoint-Span Quadratic Transport, initialized at a late preactivation checkpoint, using an independent 1,024-row pilot for the absolute center, protected Kerdock particles only for centered shape, and a fresh 2,048–4,096-point suffix cloud.

There should be two frozen configurations:

Target	Checkpoint	Nonlinear suffix	Fresh cloud	Recommended target
Primary sparse-control test	preactivation (h_{24})	8 ReLU stages	2,048	128 frozen sparse-probe anchors
Conservative layer-31 test	preactivation (h_{28})	4 ReLU stages	4,096	8 frozen layer-31 coordinates

The method is not yet submission-ready. In the synthetic experiments, the unshrunk micro-cubature estimate was not accurate enough to replace the protected center outright. Its signed error direction was nevertheless informative, and a frozen shrinkage of roughly (0.25)–(0.40) produced substantial held-out gains.

The practical verdict is therefore:

GO for a frozen oracle-to-deployable experiment; STOP if it does not reach approximately (0.60\times) baseline raw MSE on the sparse-anchor harness or reproduce the M83 center tolerance.

1. What the evidence actually requires

The layer-31 post-ReLU mean defect is the dominant repairable channel: oracle correction removed about (78.13%) of noise-corrected final MSE on 64 untouched networks, with 64/64 wins. The first two final-error PCA modes contain (54.25%) of the error energy, while roughly 8.5 modes contain 90%; layer-31 and final-error geometry have linear CKA (0.9828).
The channel is also coordinate-sparse when support is selected correctly. On the eight-network sparse oracle, 12 corrected layer-31 coordinates captured (55.23%) of the complete layer-31 oracle benefit, while 32 captured (81.39%).

The newer sparse cubic decomposition is potentially stronger still: an agent log reports that 128 exactly anchored coordinate probes reached (0.190\times) raw MSE on all eight tested networks. This is an encouraging ceiling, not yet a frozen validation result. The same experiment attributed most remaining error to lower-order sample-center terms rather than the cubic contraction itself.

The center-accuracy requirement is severe. In M83, layer-29 mean perturbations gave:

Relative mean error	Complete-control MSE ratio
0.1%	0.649
0.2%	0.690
0.3%	0.772
about 0.45%	break-even
0.65%	1.450

Current propagation is around (0.65%), so another ordinary propagated mean is not useful.

Finally, the failure mode is now clear:

Same-design cross-fitting, delete-fold jackknifing, and disjoint same-basis Edgeworth constructions landed around (1.025)–(1.030\times), after an apparent pilot gain disappeared.
Same-cloud Edgeworth anchoring was actively destructive, reaching (12.996\times) MSE.
Conditional Gaussian activation-region integration produced corrections essentially orthogonal to the signed Kerdock defect on frozen validation.

Therefore, the target cannot be “a plausible late activation distribution.” It must be an absolute, independently centered scalar or low-dimensional expectation whose signed error correlates with the protected Kerdock defect.

2. Legally and cheaply available checkpoint information
From analytic propagation

Cheap analytic state may supply:

a prior checkpoint mean (\mu_0);
diagonal variances;
approximate expected gates for the short suffix;
a low-rank or implicit covariance operator;
the exact first-layer post-ReLU mean
[
\mu_{1,j}=\frac{\lVert w_{1,j}\rVert}{\sqrt{2\pi}}.
]

The exact first-layer formula follows directly from Gaussianity.

This exact mean can also serve as a pilot control variate. Earlier width-256 experiments found that an exact-layer-1 low-rank control improved net efficiency by about (1.17\times) with 1,024 trajectories and (1.23\times) at the 2,048-trajectory optimum.

Analytic propagation should be treated as a prior, not as the final center.

From the protected Kerdock cloud

The protected cloud can legally supply only centered shape:

exact empirical diagonal variances;
covariance in 4–16 specified directions;
low-rank cross-loadings;
centered marginal third moments;
centered factor scores.

Its empirical mean must not become the absolute anchor.

For (N_K=66{,}048), width 256, and rank (r=8), a two-pass adjoint-oriented covariance sketch costs approximately:

[
2N_Kwr + 2N_Kwr + O(N_Kw)
\approx 0.58\text{B FLOPs}.
]

This avoids the roughly 8.7B cost of forming a complete empirical (256\times256) covariance by a dense cross-product.

From an independent mini-design

A 1,024-row independent pilot can supply:

absolute means in 4–16 pulled-back directions;
uncertainty from two independent 512-row halves;
diagonal skew estimates;
the (r^3) third moments within an (r\leq8) adjoint subspace;
selected bivariate contractions;
exact-layer-1 control-variate corrections.

The preferred pilot is two independently randomized antipodal orthogonal bases, or two independently scrambled 512-point Sobol-normal blocks. It must share no rotations or rows with the protected Kerdock rule.

From the adjoint

For frozen layer-31 coordinates (S), or frozen sparse probes (P), pull the target directions backward through a soft-gated suffix Jacobian:

W_{k+1}D_{k+1}\cdots W_{31}D_{31}.
]

Use the leading left singular directions of either

[
J_{k\rightarrow31}[:,S]
]

or the probe-composed map. Only these directions require accurate center and mixed-skew estimation.

This is the key dimensional reduction: the full covariance or cubic tensor may be high-dimensional, while the functional relevant to the scored output is not.

3. Three concrete algorithms
Algorithm A — Externally Centered Low-Rank Gaussian Cubature
Select a preactivation checkpoint (h_k).
Obtain an absolute center from the analytic prior plus independent pilot corrections in an (r)-dimensional adjoint span.
Estimate rank-(r) covariance loadings and exact diagonal residual variance from the centered protected cloud.
\widehat\mu_k + Lz_n + D^{1/2}\epsilon_n.
]
Propagate only through the final suffix.
Return absolute means or probe expectations.

Advantages: cheapest, cleanest Gaussian ablation, low pilot variance.

Principal bias: all skew and mixed nonlinear structure is omitted. This should be retained as the mandatory null model, not expected to win.

Independence: the protected cloud affects only (L,D). The center and integration nodes are independent, so the estimator does not algebraically reproduce the protected sample mean.

Algorithm B — Externally Centered Diagonal-Skew Mixture

Use the same center and covariance shape as Algorithm A, but replace each independent residual normal coordinate by a moment-matched quadratic-normal or two-normal mixture.

A convenient map is

\frac{z_i+b_i(z_i^2-1)}
{\sqrt{1+2b_i^2}},
]

where (b_i) is chosen to match the independently estimated standardized marginal skew.

The fresh cloud is

\widehat\mu_k + Lz_n + D^{1/2}y_n.
]

Advantages: captures the large marginal (K_{iii}) terms that proved essential in the connected-cubic branch; almost no additional forward cost.

Principal bias: ignores mixed third moments and nonlinear dependence between important factors.

Expected use: strongest candidate for 8–16 individual layer-31 coordinates at suffix depth 3–4.

Algorithm C — Externally Centered Adjoint-Span Quadratic Transport

This is the recommended algorithm.

Let (U\in\mathbb R^{256\times r}), (r\leq8), be the whitened checkpoint directions with greatest downstream sensitivity to the frozen coordinates or probes. From the independent pilot, estimate the standardized selected third cumulant

\frac1{N_p}\sum_n s_{n,a}s_{n,b}s_{n,c}.
]

Generate fresh standard-normal selected factors (z), then apply the near-identity quadratic transport

z_i+
\frac16
\sum_{a,b}
\widehat\kappa_{iab}
(z_az_b-\delta_{ab}).
]

To first order in (\kappa_3), this produces exactly the specified third cumulant. Recenter and rewhiten the generated factors to preserve the externally supplied mean and modeled covariance exactly.

Combine this with the diagonal-skew residual from Algorithm B:

\widehat\mu_k
+
L_U s
+
D^{1/2}y.
]

Then propagate the fresh cloud through the true ReLU suffix and compute only the requested anchors.

Advantages:

includes adjoint-selected bivariate and trivariate skew;
never forms a full third-order tensor;
automatically includes nonlinear interactions across the exact suffix;
targets the scalar/probe functionals rather than generic state reconstruction.

Principal bias:

fourth-order and higher discrepancies;
third moments outside the selected span;
finite-pilot error in (\widehat\kappa);
near-identity transport becomes inaccurate for very large skew.

Expected use: strongest candidate for the 128 sparse-probe center terms and for suffix depths 6–8.

4. Full pseudocode for Algorithm C
INPUT
    weights W1...W32
    protected Kerdock checkpoint preactivations Hk_K
    protected layer-31 particles A31_K
    protected final preactivations Z32_K
    frozen target:
        coordinate set S, or
        sparse probes P
    analytic prior mean mu0 at checkpoint
    parameters:
        pilot rows Np = 1024
        fresh suffix rows Ns = 2048 or 4096
        adjoint rank r = 8
        shrink alpha = 0.30

1. BUILD INDEPENDENT PILOT
    Construct two independent 512-row antipodal orthogonal/Sobol blocks.
    Propagate them from the input to checkpoint preactivation hk.
    Retain exact layer-1 activations for control variates.

2. CONSTRUCT TARGET ADJOINT SPAN
    Estimate expected gates in the short suffix from analytic state or pilot.
    Pull frozen coordinates/probes backward to checkpoint:
        B = soft_adjoint(checkpoint -> layer31, S or P)
    Weight B by checkpoint diagonal scale.
    U = leading r left singular vectors of B.
    Freeze U using weights and the frozen target only.

3. ESTIMATE ABSOLUTE CENTER
    Cross-fit a linear control from exact layer-1 activations to pilot scores hk @ U.
    Use exact E[a1] to estimate:
        t_hat = E[U^T hk]
    Set:
        mu_ext = mu0 + U (t_hat - U^T mu0)
    Do not insert mean(Hk_K) here.

4. ESTIMATE CENTERED SHAPE FROM PROTECTED CLOUD
    mK = sample_mean(Hk_K)                 # centering only
    X  = Hk_K - mK
    Y  = X @ U
    Cq = mean(Y^T Y)
    G  = mean(X^T Y)
    L  = G @ inverse_sqrt(Cq)
    D  = clip(diag_cov(X) - row_norms(L)^2, 0, infinity)

5. ESTIMATE SELECTED NON-GAUSSIAN MOMENTS
    Whiten independent pilot factor scores:
        s_pilot = whiten((Hk_pilot - mu_ext) @ U)
    kappa[a,b,c] = mean(s_a s_b s_c)

    residual_pilot =
        Hk_pilot - mu_ext - projected_factor_reconstruction
    gamma_i =
        standardized_third_moment(residual_pilot_i)

    Optionally retain only the 16–32 kappa entries with largest
    adjoint-weighted predicted contribution.

6. GENERATE FRESH MICRO-CUBATURE
    Generate 2–4 independent scrambled Sobol-normal blocks.

    For each fresh factor vector z:
        s_i = z_i
              + (1/6) sum_ab kappa[i,a,b]
                * (z_a z_b - indicator[a=b])

    Recenter and rewhiten all s nodes.

    For each diagonal residual coordinate:
        y_i = moment_matched_skew_transform(epsilon_i, gamma_i)

    Construct:
        h_node = mu_ext + s @ L^T + y * sqrt(D)

7. TRUE SHORT-SUFFIX PROPAGATION
    Starting at preactivation h_node:
        a = ReLU(h_node)
        propagate through the remaining true layers to layer 31

    Compute only:
        anchor_j = mean(a31_j), j in S
    or:
        anchor_r = mean(phi_r(a31)), r = 1...128

8. UNCERTAINTY CHECK
    Compute anchors separately for the independent Sobol blocks and pilot halves.

    Disable the correction when:
        pilot-half corrections have negative dot product, or
        block disagreement exceeds a frozen threshold, or
        estimated signal-to-noise is below a frozen minimum.

    Disagreement is only a fallback diagnostic.
    It is never used as the absolute anchor.

9. APPLY CORRECTION
    delta = anchor - protected_sample_anchor

    Use:
        corrected_anchor =
            protected_sample_anchor + alpha * delta

    For K corrected coordinates:
        update retained final preactivations by the rank-K product
            DeltaA31[:, S] @ W32[S, :]
        apply the true final ReLU and average.

    For sparse cubic probes:
        insert the absolute anchor terms directly into the frozen control.

OUTPUT
    corrected final prediction
    absolute anchor estimates
    pilot-half and cloud-block uncertainty diagnostics
5. Why this does not recreate same-cloud degeneracy

The degeneracy occurs when the same empirical cloud defines both:

the centered feature (\phi(a;\widehat\mu,\widehat\Sigma)); and
its supposed expectation.

Then the “estimated expectation minus cloud average” either vanishes identically or becomes a noisy re-expression of the same sample center.

The proposed construction breaks every part of that identity:

(\widehat\mu_k) comes from an independent pilot and an analytic prior;
the protected Kerdock cloud contributes only centered covariance shape;
third moments used in the transport come from the independent pilot;
integration nodes are newly generated;
the suffix expectation is evaluated on the new cloud;
the protected sample anchor appears only afterward, when applying the signed correction.

Symbolically, the anchor is

Q_{\mathrm{fresh}}
\left[
F_{\mathrm{suffix}}
\left(
\widehat\mu_{\mathrm{ind}}
+
T_{\widehat C_K,\widehat\kappa_{\mathrm{ind}}}(z)
\right)
\right],
]

not (Q_K[F]) or (Q_K[\phi]).

There remains statistical dependence through the centered protected shape (\widehat C_K), but there is no algebraic equality forcing (\widehat A) to reproduce the sample anchor.

A strict-independence ablation should replace (\widehat C_K) with analytic/pilot covariance. If only the hybrid version works, that suggests the centered protected shape carries useful phase-aware information; it still does not imply same-cloud degeneracy.

6. Bias and variance analysis

For a selected scalar anchor (A=E[F(h_k)]), write the fitted-state errors as

[
\delta\mu,\qquad
\delta C,\qquad
\delta\kappa_3.
]

The leading anchor bias is approximately

[
\widehat A-A
\approx
J^\top\delta\mu
+
\frac12\langle H,\delta C\rangle
+
\frac16\langle T,\delta\kappa_3\rangle
+
R_4.
]

Here (J,H,T) are derivatives of the exact suffix functional in the selected direction, and (R_4) contains fourth-order and nonlinear transport errors.

Center error

This is the binding term according to M83. Restricting estimation to an adjoint span changes the objective from full-vector relative error to

\frac{
\lVert B^\top(\widehat\mu-\mu)\rVert^2
}{
\lVert B^\top\mu\rVert^2
}.
]

Both this weighted error and complete-control MSE should be reported. The latter is authoritative.

Pilot variance

Direct pilot error scales approximately as (N_p^{-1/2}), but only (r\leq8) directional means are estimated. Scrambling, antipodal pairing, exact input moments, and the exact-layer-1 control should materially reduce the constant.

The (r^3) selected third moments have variance roughly (O(r^3/N_p)). With (r=8), only 512 entries are required.

Fresh-cloud variance

Randomized QMC reduces integration error under the fitted state, but it cannot reduce model bias. Increasing (N_s) may therefore converge more accurately to the wrong expectation. This occurred in the synthetic cloud-size sweep.

Shrinkage

Use

A_K+\alpha(\widehat A-A_K).
]

The shrinkage is not what supplies the signed anchor; (\widehat A) is already absolute. Shrinkage protects against model bias and pilot noise when applying it to the stronger protected estimator.

The synthetic optima were generally between (0.20) and (0.50). Freeze (\alpha=0.30) for the first real experiment, with (\alpha=0) as the uncertainty fallback.

Setup

width 64;
random He-initialized ReLU suffixes;

Cloud-size sweep

The nonmonotonic depth-8 result is important. More nodes reduced integration noise but exposed fitted-state bias more precisely. Cloud size must therefore be frozen from complete-control validation, not assumed to be “larger is better.”

Uncertainty experiment

Two independent 2,048-node micro-clouds frequently disagreed by order one relative to the estimated correction. A hard disagreement threshold often disabled every test network. Consequently:

two-cloud disagreement is not yet a useful continuous shrinkage estimator;
pilot-half and cloud-block disagreement should be used only as a catastrophic fallback;
the deployed shrink must be frozen globally rather than estimated separately for every network.
9. Oracle and deployable experiment protocol
Stage A — Algebra and independence tests

Require all of the following:

Replacing the independent center with the protected-cloud center reproduces the expected algebraic collapse.
Production mode rejects any shared row, rotation, or scramble between pilot and protected design.
On an exactly Gaussian checkpoint, Algorithm A matches the analytic suffix expectation within randomized-QMC error.
Algorithms B and C reduce to Algorithm A when supplied zero skew.
Moment convention tests cover preactivation versus post-ReLU state, connected versus raw moments, and tensor orientation.
Stage B — Oracle-state suffix ceiling

For each frozen network and target:

use true independent checkpoint mean;
separately add true covariance;
separately add selected true (\kappa_3);
run depths (2,3,4,6,8);
run (K=8,16,32);
run the complete 128 sparse-probe set;
compare Gaussian, diagonal-skew, and adjoint-quadratic clouds;
test (N_s=1024,2048,4096).

Continue only if selected covariance/skew materially improves over the true-center Gaussian cloud. Otherwise the center alone is the only useful object, and micro-cubature is unnecessary.

Stage C — Independent-pilot center

Replace true center information with:

analytic prior only;
independent pilot mean;
pilot plus exact-layer-1 cross-fitted control;
pilot correction restricted to the adjoint span;
full pilot mean as an ablation.

Record:

absolute weighted center error;
signed cosine with the true protected-design defect;
complete sparse-control MSE;
pilot-half disagreement;
worst network.
Stage D — Hybrid versus strict independence

Compare:

analytic/pilot covariance only;
centered protected Kerdock shape;
protected diagonal variance plus pilot factor covariance;
protected rank-8 shape plus independent skew.

This identifies whether protected centered shape is helpful without allowing it to define the absolute center.

Stage E — Frozen validation

Freeze before opening validation:

checkpoint;
suffix depth;
pilot construction;
(N_p,N_s);
rank;
target set;
quadratic terms retained;
shrinkage;
uncertainty fallback.

Use at least 24 untouched networks, followed by the 64-network holdout only after validation passes.

10. Stop/go gates
GO

Proceed toward integration only when all are true:

Complete-control result: raw MSE ratio at most approximately (0.60).
The reported exact sparse-probe ceiling is (0.190); retaining half its benefit requires
[
1-\tfrac12(1-0.190)=0.595.
]
Center precision: adjoint-weighted center error is at most (0.3%), or the independently reproduced complete-control tolerance is at least as strong as M83’s (0.772\times) result at (0.3%).
Signed prediction: frozen-validation correction cosine is positive and preferably above (0.5).
Robustness: at least 80% of networks improve, with worst candidate/baseline no worse than (1.10).
Compute: anchor plus sparse application stays below 8B additional FLOPs; composed control stays below 14B.
Independence: no anchor mean or lower-order center term is computed from the protected cloud.
STOP

Close the branch when any of the following occurs:

complete-control ratio is at or above 1 on frozen validation;
the best frozen shrink is below (0.10), meaning validation learns to turn the correction off;
mean error remains above roughly (0.45%);
a depth-6-like reversal produces a material harmful tail;
increasing cloud size changes the estimate systematically more than randomized integration uncertainty, indicating dominant model bias;
pilot halves disagree in signed target space on more than 20% of networks;
the only gain comes from selecting target coordinates using true defects;
exact added compute exceeds 8B before composing with the sparse control.
11. Final recommendation

Do not estimate the complete layer-31 mean.

The priority order should be:

128 frozen sparse-probe center terms, using (h_{24}), an eight-stage suffix, (N_p=1024), (N_s=2048), rank 8, and Algorithm C.
Eight frozen layer-31 coordinates, using (h_{28}), a four-stage suffix, (N_p=1024), (N_s=4096), rank 8, and Algorithm C with diagonal-skew residuals.
Eight final-error modes as a diagnostic and compression basis.
Never promote a full 256-dimensional mean unless the sparse targets unexpectedly fail while the full oracle remains strong.

The 128-probe target is preferred because:

its exact-anchor ceiling is strongest;
no full mean vector is required;
no full final-layer replay is required;
selected cubic and lower-order center terms are produced directly;
the estimated added cost is about 5.7B;
synthetic probe anchors benefited most from adjoint-selected mixed skew.

The four-stage, eight-coordinate configuration is the conservative fallback because it had the cleanest coordinate-level synthetic result and remains near 6.2B added compute.

The research claim at this point should be narrow:

Independent short-suffix clouds can recover useful signed late-layer center information when their absolute center comes from a separate pilot and their non-Gaussian state is restricted to downstream-sensitive directions. The mechanism is promising for sparse probes, but it has not yet demonstrated the (0.3%) real-network center accuracy required by the existing tolerance curve.
