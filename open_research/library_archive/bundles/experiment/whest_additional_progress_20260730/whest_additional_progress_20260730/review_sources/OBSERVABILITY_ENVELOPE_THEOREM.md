# Observability-envelope theorem

## Theorem

Let `R` be the square-integrable baseline error in a real Hilbert space `H`, and let `F` be the sigma-algebra generated by every observable available to a correction family. Among all square-integrable `F`-measurable corrections `g`, the unique minimum-risk correction is

\[
g_F^*=\mathbb E[R\mid F].
\]

For every admissible `g`,

\[
\mathbb E\|R-g\|^2
=
\mathbb E\|R-\mathbb E[R\mid F]\|^2
+
\mathbb E\|g-\mathbb E[R\mid F]\|^2.
\]

Consequently, the maximum error energy recoverable from the information set is

\[
H(F)=\mathbb E\|\mathbb E[R\mid F]\|^2,
\]

and the inaccessible residual energy is

\[
\mathbb E\|R\|^2-H(F).
\]

## Proof

Conditional expectation is the orthogonal projection of `R` onto the closed subspace of `F`-measurable square-integrable `H`-valued random variables. Therefore

\[
R-g=(R-P_FR)+(P_FR-g),
\]

where the two terms are orthogonal in `L^2`. Expanding the squared norm gives the stated Pythagorean identity. The second term is nonnegative and vanishes exactly at `g=P_FR`.

## Nested-information corollary

For nested information sets

\[
F_0\subseteq F_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_L,
\]

let `M_k=E[R|F_k]`. Then `(M_k)` is a Hilbert-space martingale, the increments `D_k=M_k-M_{k-1}` are pairwise orthogonal in `L^2`, and

\[
H(F_k)=H(F_0)+\sum_{j=1}^k \mathbb E\|D_j\|^2.
\]

Thus a cascade helps only when a layer adds new information about the final error. Deterministic reprocessing of an unchanged information set cannot enlarge its observability envelope.

## Finite source-span corollary

For observable correction vectors `S_1,...,S_p`, the best global linear correction is the orthogonal projection of `R` onto their `L^2` span. With population Gram matrix `G_ij=E< S_i,S_j >` and `b_i=E<R,S_i>`, the optimal coefficient vector is `G^dagger b` and the recoverable energy is `b^T G^dagger b`.

A per-instance oracle coefficient is not deployable information. It measures the larger conditional envelope obtained after revealing the target-dependent alignment.

## Scope

This theorem is exact but class-relative. It does not prove that every white-box algorithm fails. To obtain an impossibility statement, one must define the legal sigma-algebra and upper-bound `H(F)` for that sigma-algebra. The experiments in this bundle estimate envelopes for specific source families; they do not supply a universal computational lower bound.
